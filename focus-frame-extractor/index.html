3Sharpness analysis

How the score works

Each frame is converted to grayscale. With “Ignore the static scene” on, a median background is built from about 48 evenly spaced keyframes and subtracted, so fixed edges in the scene (a rail, a ledge, a scale bar) don't count. The Laplacian method then measures the second derivative at every pixel, and the Sobel method measures the edge gradient. A sharp subject produces a few very strong responses and a blurred one produces only weak ones, so the score is the mean of the strongest responses, the top fraction set by “Subject size”. The method only changes which column of scores is used, so you can switch methods after analysis without running it again. Changing the region, subject size or background option needs a new analysis.

How blocks, velocity and acceleration are calculated

The per-frame score is averaged over a sliding window (the Smoothing setting) so a single noisy frame can't split a pass in two. Frames whose smoothed score reaches the threshold are grouped into blocks; gaps up to “Merge gaps” frames long are absorbed into the surrounding block, and blocks shorter than “Min. block” are dropped. Each block's flat black bar on the chart is its average score.

The subject's position in every frame is the centroid of its strongest edges (the same pixels that set the sharpness score), locked onto the densest cluster so stray edges elsewhere are ignored. Within each block, a straight line fitted to position over time gives the average velocity, and a parabola fitted to the same points gives the average acceleration (twice its curvature). Points more than three robust standard deviations off the fit are discarded before the final fit. Positions are measured in pixels, so x is positive to the right and y is positive downward. Enter the size of one pixel to get real units. Tracking needs “Ignore the static scene” switched on.
